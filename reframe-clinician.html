Talk to a smart, anxious parent
The seven habits behind every suggestion.
1. Short and plain
Short sentences. Plain English even for complex mechanics. If it needs a second read, cut it.
2. "You" for their action, name the child for the rest
"You will tighten these two straps at night." "She will be walking on it within a week."
3. No illness-implying possessives
Not "your son's deformity." Attach the issue to the device and the goal, not the person.
4. De-jargon by default
Lead with the plain function. The clinical term is optional and never the opener.
5. Honest, pointed at the next step
Every outcome truthful and forward-facing. Never sugarcoat, never fatalistic.
6. End on an action
Answer "what do I do now": next visit, what to watch for, who to call.
7. Center the kid, not the condition
Lead with the person and what becomes possible, not the deficit.
