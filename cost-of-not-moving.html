Questions you will hear in committee

The answers, with the numbers

Won't this raise everyone's premiums?

By about 75¢ a month per insured member in this Ohio estimate, or $9 a year. New Hampshire's Insurance Department priced activity-specific prosthetic coverage for children at $0.07 to $0.21 a month.

The bill's sponsors report that states with these laws have not seen significant premium increases.

Is this a new mandate the state has to pay for under the ACA?

Prosthetic and orthotic care already fits within the ACA's rehabilitative and habilitative essential health benefit categories, so the bill can be read as clarifying existing coverage.

The 2025 federal benefit rule lets states define scope within those categories. How defrayal applies still varies by state and should be confirmed with the Department of Insurance.

Does every device user actually become active?

No, and the model doesn't assume it. Set activity to 25% and savings to $1,000 and Ohio lands near break-even. The case rests on the moderate and high scenarios staying well positive.

Community-dwelling prosthesis users commonly reach 4,000 to 5,000 steps a day, the threshold researchers use for a non-sedentary life.

Who does the bill actually reach?

Fully insured private plans. Self-funded employer plans fall under federal ERISA rules and are outside state mandates.

This model counts all commercial members, which overstates cost and savings by the same proportion. The balance between them holds.

What stops overuse?

Coverage requires a physician's finding of medical necessity. The sponsors say the bill keeps current replacement-frequency rules in place.

Average prosthetic cost runs above $13,000 per device (GAO), so the barrier today is cost, not demand.
